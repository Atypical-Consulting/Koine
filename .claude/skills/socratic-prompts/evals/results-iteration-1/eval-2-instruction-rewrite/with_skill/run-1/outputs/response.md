Here's that instruction rewritten as a Socratic prompt — it makes the model define what a *reply-worthy* cold email is, reason with real cold-outreach frameworks, and only then write, instead of pattern-matching to the usual "I hope this email finds you well" slop:

**Cold outreach email — reason first, then write**
> What actually makes a cold B2B email earn a reply rather than a delete or a spam-flag? Separate the levers: *relevance* (does it prove you understand the recipient's world before asking for anything?), *credibility* (why should a stranger trust this?), *the single ask* (one low-friction next step, not a pitch deck), and *the subject line that earns the open*. Which frameworks apply — PAS (Problem · Agitate · Solve), AIDA, the "give value before you ask" / trigger-event angle, and the rule of one clear CTA?
> Where does this trade off: deep per-prospect research (high reply rate, doesn't scale) vs. templated volume (scales, reads generic) — and how short can it get before it loses the credibility that earns the reply?
> Now apply all of that to write a cold email for `[my B2B SaaS product]`, targeting `[buyer persona / job title]` at `[type of company]`, opening with the single `[pain or trigger event]` it removes, citing `[one proof point — metric, customer, or result]`, and closing on one low-commitment ask.

**Fill in before you run it:** `[my B2B SaaS product]`, `[buyer persona / job title]`, `[type of company]`, `[pain or trigger event]`, and `[one proof point]`. The more concrete those are, the sharper the email — especially the pain/trigger and the proof point, which are what separate a reply-worthy cold email from a generic one.

**The reusable skeleton**, so you can build more of these yourself:
> *What makes an effective `[type of output]`?* → *Which principles / frameworks apply?* → *Now apply them to `[your specific task]`.*
